Beam: Quiet Circuits — Privacy Policy

Last updated 3 September 2026. Beam: Quiet Circuits is the name on the App Store; the app appears as Beam on your home screen. This policy covers both, because they are the same app.

Beam collects a small amount of anonymous usage data, so we can see which circuits are too hard, too easy, or where people stop playing. It is on by default, and you can switch it off at any time in the app's settings.

What's collected: which circuits you open, solve or leave, roughly how long each takes and how many moves it costs, and basic app events such as opening the app or changing a setting.

What's not collected: your name, email address, or any other personal detail — Beam has no account to attach one to. There is no advertising and no ad network, no cross-app or device tracking, and no location of any kind, precise or otherwise.

The usage data is tagged with a random identifier generated on your device the first time you install the app — not your device's own identifier, and never shared with any other app. It is processed by PostHog, an analytics service, on their EU servers.

Switching analytics off in settings stops all of this immediately: nothing further is collected, and the random identifier already on your device is deleted. Switching it back on starts a fresh identifier — there is no way to reconnect it to anything collected before.

Separately, your progress — which circuits you have finished, in how many turns, and your sound, haptics, motion and palette settings — is stored on your device, in the app's own storage, and is never sent anywhere. Deleting the app deletes it.

Beam is not directed at children specifically, and nothing it collects identifies who is playing.

If this ever changes — if a future version collects anything beyond what is described here — this page will be updated before that version ships, and the change will be described here rather than implied.
